Reading these words, I suddenly felt my eyes well up.

These days I've been thinking about what pursuing graduate school really means. Watching classmates busy themselves contacting advisors and preparing applications, I've been following along. But a voice inside keeps asking: is this the path I truly want? Or am I just running because everyone else is?

At 22, life's choices are laid out before me, yet I haven't even fully understood the questions. Which school, which advisor, which research direction — every decision feels like a fork in the road that could determine the rest of my life. I'm afraid of choosing wrong, afraid of falling behind, afraid that if I don't run fast enough I'll be left in the dust.

But "life has no universal progress bar." Why must I find all the answers at 22? Some go straight to work, some continue their studies, some take a gap year to travel — every choice is a valid way to live. What matters isn't who gets there first, but where I actually want to go.

Wang Hong won the Fields Medal after years of deep dedication. She must have had moments of doubt and hesitation too, but she kept her own rhythm. Graduate school isn't the destination — it's just the starting point of one chapter. Even if I can't see clearly where I'll be in ten years, as long as the direction is upward, what's the harm in going a little slower?

Perhaps I should learn to embrace this uncertainty, to treat it as part of growing up. Choosing graduate school isn't about fearing the real world — it's because I still want to spend a few more years deepening my knowledge in this field, searching for answers a while longer. Maybe three years from now I still won't have found "life's answers," but at least I'll be closer to them.

Keep your own rhythm. Take it slow. There's no rush.
